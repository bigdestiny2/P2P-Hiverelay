01 · Executive findingsHardware is easy; coordination is hard

Under a text-oriented social workload with 5× cell replication and 180-day retention, 1,000 operators are a large over-provision of raw capacity for ~16k combined DAU. The binding constraints are rate limits, writer-group ceilings, client selection skew, and operator P&L — not terabytes or GHz.

What scales cleanly Disk, CPU, and average bandwidth at text-social mix. A Pi 5 or $5–20 VPS holds a fair-share Core role. Network physical corpus stays under ~1 TB network-wide at year-1 baseline — trivial for 1,000 nodes.
What does not scale by default Shared-NAT browser rate limits (measured 1,200 req/60s/IP knee), OutboxLog’s 20k ever-writer group ceiling, single-ingress Peerit, and mid-card economics that leave operators underwater vs VPS cost.

03 · Model & methodWhat we simulated

Deterministic closed-form accounting with a power-law traffic split — not a discrete-event packet simulator and not production evidence. Answers: “Are we order-of-magnitude fine?”

Figure 3 — Accounting pipeline.

logical_bytes_day = Σ_app (DAU × write_frac × events_per_writer × cells_per_event × avg_cell_bytes × mailbox_oh) physical_bytes_day = logical_bytes_day × R retained_physical = physical_bytes_day × retention_days fair_share_disk = retained_physical / N_operators RSS ≈ base_runtime + index(hot_set) + connection_buffers Phase2_sats ≈ disk_GB×50 + bw_GB×100 + contracted_hours×5
Grounding constants
• Blind cell size classes: 4 KiB / 16 KiB / 64 KiB / 256 KiB / 1 MiB
• Peerit soak: local OutboxLog ~140 MB RSS @ 30 clients; atomic two-relay 200 writers → ~285 MB RSS, p99 issues when co-located
• OutboxLog hard cap: 20,000 groups (ever-writers); second relay does not raise the ceiling
• Shared-NAT default: 1,200 accepted requests / 60s / IP (configurable; measured knee at 30 concurrent writers on one IP)
• ECONOMICS.md mid card: storage 50 sats/GB-mo, bandwidth 100 sats/GB, availability 5 sats/hr; 1 sat ≈ $0.001 @ $100k BTC
• Replica target R=5; retention 180 days; Peerit write frac 15%; 2.5 posts + 4 comments/writer/day; class-3 mix + 15% mailbox overhead

09 · Shared NAT deep diveThe product cliff that hardware won’t fix

Peerit CAPACITY.md measured a hard knee: with the default 1,200 accepted requests / 60 seconds / source IP, 30 concurrent writers behind one IP hit HTTP 429 during vote publication. Raising the envelope to 12,000/60s removed the knee locally — but production must choose an explicit operator policy behind a real trusted proxy.

Figure 9 — Shared-NAT policy knee from CAPACITY.md diagnostic sweeps (local two-relay).

Year-1 implication for 10k DAU If a non-trivial fraction of users are browsers behind CGNAT/office NAT, default per-IP buckets will 429 long before disk or CPU care. Fix is multi-IP edge topology, higher operator envelopes, Pear/native clients that do not collapse onto one public IP, and honest trusted-proxy headers.
Engine vs policy Distributed-profile soaks (unique IP per writer) pass correctness at 50–200 writers. The 200-writer co-located p99 miss is fixture queueing (Little’s law), not proof the journal cannot hit 2s p99 on isolated hosts. Staging isolation remains required.

Three-prong year-1 answer (from OPERATOR-INCENTIVES-Y1)

Trojan horse
Self-host Bitcoin/LN boxes already paying power — zero marginal cost. Funnel targets thousands of active relays.
Bootstrap subsidy
Time-bounded founder subsidy (e.g. 1 BTC budget class) quality-gated on uptime/challenges — not a token.
Foundation net
Always-on multi-region founder nodes as operator-of-last-resort for app credibility.
Economic implication 1,000 operators at this demand is supply-rich — healthy for decentralization, harsh for operator P&L. Expect: (a) most ops on zero-marginal home boxes, (b) paid density concentrates on contracts + head of power-law, (c) mid-card prices rise with real demand, (d) Services (AI/identity) is a separate higher-margin business, not Core.

Who pays whom (healthy year-1 pattern)

  • App publishers (Peerit et al.) buy multi-region seeding + blind capacity + optional gateway edges.
  • End users usually pay $0 directly for Core relay (app-funded or community-funded).
  • Home operators contribute spare capacity for reputation / ideology / future allocation.
  • Professional operators concentrate where paid contracts exist (launch apps, enterprise).

13 · Engineering gapsWhat must be true before this scenario is real

  1. Blind substrate production-ready — not draft packages; soak + multi-node evidence (BLIND-CAPACITY-SIZING is planning-only).
  2. Peerit dual-read → blind-first — or accept OutboxLog’s 20k-writer ceiling forever.
  3. Multi-ingress / multi-operator Peerit — single Outbox writer is a social SPOF.
  4. Rate-limit architecture for browsers — shared NAT is a measured product issue, not theoretical.
  5. Client selection that spreads load — avoid 50 operators doing 95% of work (or accept paid-tier concentration deliberately).
  6. T1 gateway fleet admission freeze — public shells at scale without transitional allowlists only.
  7. Media policy — if images/video dominate, re-run this model with class-4/5 and CDN-like BW.
  8. Phase-2 settlement path — Lightning/micropayments still pending; year-1 is reputation + subsidy designed.

14 · RecommendationsWhat to optimize for this year-1 shape

Product
Ship multi-path Peerit (browser + Pear). Treat 10k DAU as a rate-limit + multi-ingress problem first, disk second. Don’t wait for media to force a redesign of writer caps.
Operators
Publish a T1 VPS recipe (2–4 GB RAM, ~100 GB disk) and a home/Pi recipe. Recruit via zero-marginal self-host channels. Don’t require enterprise iron for Core social.
Economics
Expect Phase-1 reputation for most of 1k ops. Price paid capacity above mid-card if you want professional density. Keep Services (AI) economically separate from Core commodity.
Bottom line A year-1 world with 10k Peerit DAU and 1,000 operators is hardware-easy and coordination-hard. The system scales on paper; the work is making selection, payments, multi-operator truth, and browser rate-limit architecture work in production without collapsing back into a few founder boxes.
